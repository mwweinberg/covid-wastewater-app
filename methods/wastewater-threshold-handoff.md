## Goal and scope

Develop a method (and Python code) that turns Berlin wastewater data into an estimated background prevalence of SARS-CoV-2, and from that into thresholds for deciding when unmasked indoor activities are acceptable.

The output of this project is **only the threshold calculation**: the method, the fitted parameters, and the code needed to compute it. It will be handed to a separate Claude instance that maintains an existing Android app (https://github.com/mwweinberg/covid-wastewater-app — not the user's repo; the user is not forking or maintaining it). The app's data pipeline is Python (`pipeline/build_data.py`), so deliver Python.

The user's personal risk tolerance is an input the user sets; the code should take it as a parameter, not hard-code it.

## How we got here (short version)

1. **Starting idea:** use a published personal cutoff (Robert Wachter, Washington Post, 18 Feb 2022: unmask in low-risk indoor settings at ~10 reported cases/100k/day with test positivity under 1–2%), convert it to prevalence, then map prevalence onto wastewater.
2. **Converting Wachter's cutoff:** with 2–6× ascertainment and ~7 days infectious, it implies ~0.14–0.42% prevalence (central ~0.28%). His framing ("person in line with me", "sitting near me") is about the 1–5 people near you: at 0.28%, a 0.28–1.4% chance that at least one of them is infectious (corrected 2026-11-01; the earlier "roughly 1–2%" was arithmetically wrong). He explicitly kept masking in crowded spaces.
3. **Rejected approach:** calibrating wastewater against 2022 Berlin case data. The error budget compounds (ascertainment ~3× wide, infectious period ~1.4×, two lab-change bridges, variant shedding drift), plausibly ~10× total. That is about as wide as the gap between a typical low and a typical high week in the current Berlin series, so the threshold would be nearly useless. Keep 2022 only as a sanity check.
4. **Chosen approach:** calibrate within the current lab period (since April 2024) against a current independent incidence estimate built from GrippeWeb (population ARE incidence) × sentinel SARS-CoV-2 PCR positivity. Add a calibration-free relative rule on top.
5. **Data access verified (2026-11-01):** all sources usable. Sentinel positivity is national only (no machine-readable regional data); a Berlin-vs-national comparison showed national positivity is an acceptable stand-in (see Validation log).

## Core formulas

- Prevalence ≈ weekly incidence × (days infectious ÷ 7) ÷ symptomatic fraction
  - With ~7 days infectious, prevalence per 100k ≈ new infections per 100k per week.
  - Run both 7 and 10 days infectious.
  - Steady-state approximation; less accurate when incidence changes fast.
- Chance at least one of N people is infectious: P = 1 − (1 − p)^N
- Inverse, prevalence ceiling for tolerance P_max: p_max = 1 − (1 − P_max)^(1/N)
- Reference values at p = 0.28%: N=1 → 0.28%, N=5 → 1.4%, N=25 → 6.8%, N=50 → 13%.
- Being in a room with an infectious person ≠ infection. Duration, ventilation, distance matter. Thresholds should differ by activity type.

### 1. AMELAG wastewater (RKI / UBA)

- Repo: https://github.com/robert-koch-institut/Abwassersurveillance_AMELAG
- Site-level file: `amelag_einzelstandorte.tsv` (raw: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/robert-koch-institut/Abwassersurveillance_AMELAG/main/amelag_einzelstandorte.tsv`)
- National aggregate: `amelag_aggregierte_kurve.tsv`
- Documentation: `Readme.en.md` in the repo
- Columns: `standort, bundesland, datum, viruslast, viruslast_normalisiert, vorhersage, obere_schranke, untere_schranke, einwohner, laborwechsel, typ, unter_bg`
- Filter `typ == "SARS-CoV-2"`.

**Use `viruslast_normalisiert`.** For SARS-CoV-2, AMELAG normalizes by flow: concentration × (flow during sampling ÷ median flow). For a fixed plant this is proportional to viral load per capita.

**Caution on `vorhersage`:** RKI's README contradicts itself on whether this GAM fit uses normalized or non-normalized values. Do your own smoothing on `viruslast_normalisiert` instead.

**Below the limit of quantification:** values below LOQ are reported as 0.5 × LOQ; `unter_bg == "ja"` flags samples where at least half the target genes were below LOQ. This flattens the low end, which is where the threshold will sit. Handle explicitly (exclude, or treat as an upper bound "≤ value").

**The `unter_bg` flag is not reliable for Berlin (checked 2026-11-01).** Since 2024-04, 16 Berlin samples (Ruhleben 8, Schönerlinde 6, Waßmannsdorf 2), all between 2026-04-22 and 2026-06-21, have raw `viruslast` of exactly 1,000 copies/L, the most frequent single raw value in the Berlin series. All 16 are flagged `unter_bg == "nein"`. Only 1 Berlin sample since 2024-04 carries `unter_bg == "ja"` (Ruhleben 2026-03-25, raw > 1,000; 11 across the full history since 2022). Nationally since 2024-04: 32 SARS-CoV-2 samples have raw exactly 1,000; 12 flagged "ja", 20 "nein". Interpretation: 1,000 copies/L is very likely a reporting floor (probably 0.5 × LOQ) that the flag misses. **Detect below-LOQ samples by the raw value (`viruslast == 1000`, and re-check for other repeated floor values on refresh), not by the flag.** These weeks affect the fit: in the Berlin-vs-national check (Validation log), including the 7 floor-affected weeks moved the log-log slope from 1.01 to 1.12 and the 2026-H1 offset from −0.04 to −0.18 log10.

**Sampling frequency:** RKI's README says twice weekly (24-h composite samples, Mon–Tue and Wed–Thu). Berlin since 2024-04-03: 214–230 samples per plant (~1.7/week). Data current through 2026-10-11 when checked.

**National aggregate (`amelag_aggregierte_kurve.tsv`):** weekly; RKI method: weekly mean of log10 values per site → per site-laboratory combination, mean deviation from the cross-site weekly mean is subtracted (this absorbs lab changes) → population-weighted mean, only for weeks with ≥10 sites. Recent coverage ~24% of the German population (`anteil_bev`). **It includes Berlin at ~19% of its population weight** (3.9M of ~20M connected), so do not compare Berlin against it directly; rebuild a national curve excluding Berlin from the site file using the same method (done in the Validation log analysis).

## Method (steps)

0. **Data access.** Load AMELAG and GrippeWeb. *(Done 2026-11-01: Diagrams page not usable; PDF scraper `pipeline/sentinel.py` built and validated, national Tab. 1 counts 2023-W39 … 2026-W43 in `data/sentinel_tab1.csv`. See Data sources §3 and the Validation log.)*
1. **Berlin wastewater series (current lab period).** *(Done 2026-11-01: `pipeline/wastewater.py` → `data/berlin_wastewater_weekly.csv`, parameters in `data/berlin_wastewater_params.json`. See Validation log.)* From 2024-04-03. `viruslast_normalisiert`, log10, weekly mean per plant, population-weighted geometric mean across the three plants, light smoothing (2–3 weeks). Handle below-LOQ values explicitly, detecting them by the raw value (`viruslast == 1000`), not only `unter_bg` (see Data sources §1).
   **Below-LOQ decision (2026-11-01):**
   - *Detection:* a sample is below LOQ if `unter_bg == "ja"` or raw `viruslast` equals a known floor value (currently 1,000 copies/L; re-check for other repeated floor values on each refresh).
   - *Weekly series:* a week containing any below-LOQ sample gets the flag `below_loq = True` and is kept in the series as "at or below the quantification floor".
   - *Fit (step 4):* `below_loq` weeks are excluded. Sensitivity check: refit with the below-LOQ samples kept at their reported value (1,000 copies/L, i.e. RKI's 0.5 × LOQ convention), and report how much the slope, intercept and thresholds move.
   - *Runtime:* ~~a `below_loq` week counts as below any threshold that lies above the floor.~~ **Corrected 2026-11-01 (review finding A4, user-approved):** a week is classified by its value like any other week. The `below_loq` flag is used only to omit the trend line and to exclude weeks from the fit. (The old rule would have put 2026-W13, 18,000 copies/L with one flagged sample of six, below the threshold whatever its value.) If a threshold ever lies at or below the floor, wastewater can't resolve it, and the method note must say so.
   - *Why not a regression that treats these values as upper bounds* (in statistics, a "censored-data" or Tobit-type regression; "censored" there means "only a bound is known"): it's statistically cleaner, but it's complicated by weekly values that average below-LOQ and normal samples across three plants, and gains little over exclusion for so few affected weeks (8 below-LOQ weeks so far; with the trailing 2-week window they remove 12 weeks from the fit; corrected 2026-11-01). The sentinel anchor for those weeks has only 0–1 positives anyway.
   - *Alternatives rejected:* substituting a value as the main method, because it treats an unknown value between 0 and ~LOQ (the reported 1,000 is a placeholder at 0.5 × LOQ) as exact. *(Corrected 2026-11-01, review finding C11: the earlier added reason "flattens the low end" is contradicted by our own data. In the Berlin-vs-national check, the floor weeks sit below the national-predicted level, and including them steepened the slope from 1.01 to 1.12. The decision stands on the first reason alone.)* If a plant is missing in a week, a plain weighted mean shifts with plant-level offsets; adjust for per-plant offsets the way RKI's aggregation does (subtract each plant's mean deviation from the cross-plant weekly mean before weighting).
2. **Anchor (symptomatic incidence).** *(Done 2026-11-01: `pipeline/anchor.py` → `data/anchor_weekly.csv`. See Validation log.)* Weekly incidence = GrippeWeb `Osten` ARE incidence × national sentinel SARS-CoV-2 positivity. Model positivity as positives out of samples (binomial), smoothed across weeks, not raw weekly percentages. If the ILI/non-ILI split becomes available, use ILI incidence × ILI positivity + non-ILI incidence × non-ILI positivity (the RKI formula). Assumptions: sentinel positivity applies to everyone in the population with ARE (sentinel patients are probably sicker than average; bias direction unknown); the national positivity curve stands in for Berlin. *(Corrected 2026-11-01, review finding C9: what was checked is Berlin vs national **wastewater** and `Osten` vs national **GrippeWeb**, not positivity itself, which isn't available for Berlin. Wastewater showed amplitude ~1:1 and level drift ≤ ~×1.6 between half-years; cross-correlation is best at lag 0, but wave peaks show Berlin 1 week (2025) and 2 weeks (2026) ahead of national; see Validation log.)* A constant Berlin-vs-national level offset in positivity cannot be detected with the available data and is an untested assumption. *(Added 2026-11-01, review finding C10: this differs from RKI's GW-VPR-I, which is for adults with an ILI/non-ILI split. We use all ages, because `Osten` has only `00+`. Children are ~25% of ARE episodes (Bundesweit 2024–26, assuming 14% of the population is aged 0–14), and the sentinel's age mix is unknown. Rough bias estimate ±15%.)*
3. **Convert to prevalence.** × (days infectious ÷ 7); ÷ symptomatic fraction.
   **Decision (2026-11-01):**
   - *Symptomatic fraction:* best guess 0.60, i.e. the share of infections that cause fever, cough or sore throat (GrippeWeb's ARE definition) and get reported as a new illness. The plausible range is 0.40–0.80; the evidence is in the Validation log, "Symptomatic fraction literature check".

### 2026-11-01: Incidence anchor (`pipeline/anchor.py`, step 2)

**Construction.**
- *Formula:* symptomatic SARS-CoV-2 incidence per 100k per week = GrippeWeb `Osten` ARE incidence (`00+`) × national sentinel SARS-CoV-2 positivity.
- *Positivity:* positives and samples are summed over a centered 3-week window (weeks t−1, t, t+1), then divided.
  - A centered window is fine here because the anchor is only used to fit on past data, never in real time.
  - At the ends of the series the window has 2 weeks; these rows have `edge_of_series = True`.
  - The 95% interval is a Jeffreys interval, Beta(x+0.5, n−x+0.5), which stays sensible when there are 0 positives.
- *95% interval for incidence:* Monte Carlo, 20,000 draws per week, fixed seed. It combines:
  - the positivity posterior;
  - an approximate binomial interval for the ARE rate, with the number of GrippeWeb reports as sample size. This ignores RKI's survey weighting, so it understates that part, which is small anyway.

  **It covers sampling noise only, not the systematic assumptions** (sentinel patients sicker than average, `Osten` and national data standing in for Berlin, no ILI/non-ILI split). Those belong in the step 3 band.
- *Output:* `data/anchor_weekly.csv`, 2023-W39 … 2026-W43. For each week it has the raw inputs, the weekly and pooled positivity with interval, and the incidence with interval.

**Results, calibration period 2024-W14 … 2026-W43 (134 weeks).**
- ARE incidence: median 5,447 per 100k per week (range 1,697–9,353).
- Pooled positivity: median 2.3% (0–20.9%). **13 weeks have 0 positives even over the 3-week window**, so their incidence estimate is 0, with an upper limit only.
- Symptomatic SARS-CoV-2 incidence: median 119 per 100k per week (0–1,754). Peaks: 1,736 (2024-W42), 1,754 (2025-W49), 213 (2026-W35). (2026-W35 itself had 0 positives; its "peak" comes from pooling with W34 and W36, which had 1 and 4 positives. Noted 2026-11-01.)
- Interval width (high ÷ low): median ×5.0, 90th percentile ×46, maximum ×5,980 (the zero-positive weeks). The low end is essentially unconstrained by the anchor, as expected.

**Sanity check against the Berlin wastewater series.** Exploratory only, not the step 4 fit: log10 incidence vs log10 wastewater, excluding below-LOQ and zero-positive weeks, ~113 weeks.
- Correlation: with `log10_trailing2`, r = 0.87 at lag 0, 0.84 at lag 1, 0.80 at lag 2. Unsmoothed: 0.85, 0.84, 0.81. The best match is at lag 0, so wastewater doesn't visibly lead the anchor at weekly resolution, partly because the anchor's 3-week pooling smears timing.
- Slope: 1.02 at lag 0 (trailing2), so a single conversion factor (slope 1) looks plausible. To be tested properly in step 4.
- *Residual scatter* of log10(incidence ÷ wastewater): SD 0.30 log10 (×2.0) per week, which includes the anchor's sampling noise.
- *Drift by quarter* (mean residual relative to the overall mean): −0.34 to +0.22 log10. Ignoring 2026-Q2, which has only 2 usable weeks (+0.66), the anchor reads low in 2025-Q1/Q2 (−0.34, −0.29) and high in 2025-Q4 (+0.22).
  - No consistent seasonal pattern yet.
  - One possible mechanism, not tested: in flu-dominated winter weeks, sentinel swabbing may under-represent SARS-CoV-2 patients.
  - Drift of this size (about ×2 either way) needs to be reflected in the uncertainty band; step 4 fits seasons separately to check it.
- The small 2026 wave is consistent in both series: wastewater ~5.0 log10 in Sep–Oct 2026 vs ~5.9 at the 2024 peak, and incidence ~100–200 vs ~1,700 per 100k.

**Implications for step 4.**
- Zero-positive weeks can't be log-transformed. Fit with a count-based likelihood (binomial positives given wastewater, scaled by ARE) instead of regressing log point estimates; failing that, exclude those weeks and say so.
- The anchor's interval varies about 1,000-fold between weeks, so the fit must weight weeks by their precision; an unweighted regression would be dominated by noisy lull weeks.
- Lag: test 0–2 weeks, but expect weak resolution (see above).

### 2026-11-01: Symptomatic fraction literature check (step 3)

**What the fraction must mean here.** The share of SARS-CoV-2 infections that a GrippeWeb participant would report as a new acute respiratory illness under GrippeWeb's ARE definition: fever OR cough OR sore throat. A runny nose alone doesn't count. This is narrower than "share with any symptom". RKI's own GrippeWeb × sentinel method (Loenenbach et al. 2024) makes no adjustment for asymptomatic infection; it estimates symptomatic illness only.

**Evidence.**

| Source | Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Omicron meta-analysis, *Vaccines* 2022 (PMC9321237) | 8 studies, 7,640 Omicron-positive people | 32.4% asymptomatic; a second review cited in PMC11215535 gives 25.5% (95% CI 17.0–38.2%); both very heterogeneous |
| REACT-1, England, rounds 17–19 (early 2022, BA.1/BA.2) | Random community sample, PCR regardless of symptoms, symptoms in the past week | ~72–76% of positives reported any of 26 symptoms (26.7–29.5% none, by round) |
| ONS Winter COVID Infection Study, England/Scotland, Nov 2023–Mar 2024 | Routine lateral-flow test every 4 weeks; symptoms in the 7 days before the test | Among positives: cough 73.8%, sore throat 64.6%, fever 29.0%, runny nose 86.1%; no overall "any symptom" share published |
| Netherlands vaccine-effectiveness cohort, Oct 2023–Jan 2024 (PMC10986669) | Self-tested infections | 68% symptomatic, 21% asymptomatic, 11% unknown |
| Coastal Kenya, Nov 2023–Mar 2024 (XBB/JN.1) | Community testing regardless of symptoms | ~83% asymptomatic, i.e. ~16% symptomatic (younger population, different setting) |

**Reasoning for 0.40–0.80 (best guess 0.60).**
- *Upper 0.80:* in the Winter study, the share of positives with fever, cough or sore throat combined is probably ~85–90%. But rapid tests preferentially detect symptomatic, high-viral-load infections, so 0.80 is a cap.
- *Best guess 0.60:* REACT-1's random PCR sample found ~72–76% with any symptom; the subset with fever, cough or sore throat is lower, roughly 0.55–0.65.
- *Lower 0.40:* allows for mild symptoms not reported to GrippeWeb as an illness, and for milder repeat infections in a highly immune population. The Kenyan result shows much lower values are possible in other settings. This bound rests on the weakest evidence.
- *Not verified:* REACT-1 per-round figures come from round reports and summaries, not the paper's tables; the Winter study's combined fever/cough/sore-throat share is an inference from the single-symptom figures.

**Further judgment calls.**
- Asymptomatic infections are counted as fully infectious. Studies suggest they transmit less, so this makes the estimate cautious (prevalence of *infectious* people is somewhat overstated).
- The best guess drives decisions (user decision, 2026-11-01); see Method step 3.

### 2026-11-01: Days infectious literature check (step 3)

**What the number must mean.** The average total time a person can infect others, including the days before symptoms, so that weekly new infections × (days ÷ 7) gives the share infectious at once.

**Current variants.** All circulating variants still descend from Omicron. RKI's report for KW 41/2026: XFG, a recombinant of the Omicron sublineages LF.7 and LP.8.1.2 and a WHO variant under monitoring since June 2025, is most common, followed by JN.1 and its sublineages (a WHO variant of interest) and PQ.16.1.1. The relevant change since the early Omicron studies is population immunity, which is now much higher and mostly hybrid (vaccination plus infection).

**Evidence.**

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Boucau et al., *NEJM* 2022 (PMC9258747); 34 BA.1 outpatients, 2021–22, 35% boosted | Culturable virus for a median 8 days (IQR 5–10) from symptom onset or first positive test, whichever came first; 5 days (IQR 3–9) from first positive test. No large differences by vaccination status. |
| Jang et al., *Open Forum Infect Dis* 2022; 11 Omicron patients, Korea, Dec 2021 | Culture positive up to day 8 after symptom onset; negative from about day 9 |
| Manica et al., *Lancet Reg Health Eur*; household study (from search summary, paper not opened) | Serial interval ~2.4 days; ~51% of transmission before symptom onset |
| German household study (PMC9530378) | Mean serial interval 3.6 days for Omicron (4.8 wild type) |
| US household preprint 2024 (medRxiv 2024.10.10.24315246) | Mean generation time 3.5 days for Omicron overall, including XBB |

**Reasoning for 7 days (range 5–10).**
- *Upward:* about 8 days of culturable virus after symptom onset, plus ~1–2 days before symptoms, suggests up to ~10 days.
- *Downward:*
  - Culture positivity overstates real-world transmissibility, because the late days have low viral load. The ~3.5-day generation time shows transmission is concentrated around symptom onset.
  - Population immunity is now much higher than in the 2021–22 cohorts.
  - Many symptomatic people stay home, which shortens the time infectious people are out in public, the quantity that matters for this tool.
- *Best guess 7 days:* somewhat cautious given the downward arguments; also the original plan's base case.
- *Limitation:* all culture studies are BA.1-era; none found for current variants.

### 2026-11-01: Wastewater → prevalence fit (`pipeline/fit.py`, step 4)

**Model.** log10(prevalence_t) = a + b × (w_{t−L} − 5).
- *w:* Berlin wastewater, log10 copies/L, trailing 2-week mean (computable in real time).
- *L:* the lag in weeks.
- *Reference point:* 5 = 100,000 copies/L, so 10^a is the predicted prevalence at 100,000 copies/L.

**Fitting method.** The model is fitted to the raw weekly sentinel counts, not to log prevalence estimates:
- Each candidate (a, b) implies a positivity for each week: prevalence ÷ 1.667 ÷ that week's GrippeWeb ARE rate.
- The fit chooses the (a, b) under which the observed positives, out of the swabs taken, are most likely.
- A beta-binomial distribution allows for more week-to-week variation than pure chance (extra-variation parameter κ fitted).
- Uncertainty comes from a moving-block bootstrap: blocks of 6 consecutive weeks, 500 replicates, seed 20261101.
- *Plain-language explanation given to the user:* "try a candidate conversion number, work out how many positives it predicts each week, compare with what the lab found, keep the candidate that matches best"; weeks with 0 positives still count, and weeks with more swabs count more.

**Weeks used.**
- 119 calibration-period weeks (2024-W15 … 2026-W41) with wastewater, GrippeWeb and sentinel data.
- Excluded: weeks whose trailing 2-week wastewater includes a below-LOQ week.
- Lag comparisons use the 113 weeks common to all lags.

**Model choice rule** (fixed before results): the lag with the best AIC among free-slope fits; the slope stays at 1 unless the free slope improves AIC by more than 2.

**Comparison (113 common weeks).**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | free | −2.897 | 1.227 | 0.127% | **512.7** |
| 0 | 1 | −2.728 | 1 | 0.187% | 519.2 |
| 1 | free | −2.811 | 1.116 | 0.155% | 539.4 |
| 1 | 1 | −2.726 | 1 | 0.188% | 539.4 |
| 2 | free | −2.689 | 0.943 | 0.205% | 570.7 |

**Chosen: lag 0, free slope** (slope 1 is worse by ΔAIC 6.5). Refitted on all 119 usable weeks:
- a = −2.892 (95% bootstrap CI −3.059 … −2.751): **prevalence at 100,000 copies/L = 0.128% (0.087–0.177%)**.
- b = 1.220 (1.030–1.414).

**Interpretation.**
- *Lag 0:* wastewater doesn't measurably lead the GrippeWeb × sentinel estimate at weekly resolution. The trailing smoother already uses the previous week, and the ARE definition counts illness onset, which comes after infection.
- *Slope > 1:* prevalence rises somewhat faster than wastewater. A tenfold rise in wastewater means about a 17-fold rise in prevalence. Possible reasons, not tested:
  - background shedding, e.g. from long-lasting infections, keeps wastewater from falling as far as prevalence;
  - sentinel or GrippeWeb behaviour that differs between quiet and busy periods.
  The slope CI barely excludes 1.

**Checks.**
- *Lag robustness:* lag 0 wins by a wide margin (ΔAIC 27 to lag 1).
- *Sensitivity runs:*
  - below-LOQ weeks included: b = 1.17, 0.141% at 100k;
  - unsmoothed wastewater: b = 1.17, 0.136%.
  Both are close to the chosen model.
- *Straight-line adequacy:* weeks binned into sixths by wastewater level. Observed ÷ expected positives: 1.72, 0.53, 0.94, 0.89, 1.05, 1.02 from lowest to highest.
  - The two lowest bins rest on few positives (11 and 13).
  - A curved (quadratic) model fits somewhat better (AIC 517.9 vs 523.0) but differs from the straight line by ≤ ~15% between 0.1% and 1% prevalence (e.g. at 200,000 copies/L: 0.26% vs 0.30%).
  - **Straight line kept, a judgment call made after seeing the results.** The pre-set model choice rule covered only lag and slope = 1 vs free slope; curved models weren't part of it. (An earlier summary to the user wrongly said the rule required the straight line; corrected 2026-11-01.) Reasons:
    - The difference from the curve is small next to the scatter (×1.4) and the season drift (×1.5).
    - The curve's advantage rests mostly on the sparse low end.
    - A straight line maps each wastewater level to exactly one prevalence and back.
    Revisit if the threshold region moves to the extremes. Open for user review.
- *Season drift (slope held at 1.22, level free; reproducible from `fit.py` since 2026-11-01, rows `season_*` with slope "chosen" in `data/fit_comparison.csv`. For 2023/24 the optimizer reports non-convergence because the extra-variation term runs to "none", but the level is stable).* A "season" is RKI's respiratory season, calendar week 40 to week 39 of the next year, roughly October to September; e.g. 2024/25 = Oct 2024 – Sep 2025. Results: prevalence at 100k copies/L was 0.118% in 2023/24 (Apr–Sep 2024, 25 weeks), 0.115% in 2024/25 (52 weeks) and 0.170% in 2025/26 (40 weeks).

  - Per-season free slopes are consistent: 1.25, 1.32, 1.24.
  - 2026/27 is not yet fitted (2 weeks).

### 2026-11-01: Trend rule test (`analysis/trend_rule.py`)

**Rule family.**
- change = level(t) ÷ level(t − k), where level is the geometric mean of the last k weekly wastewater values, so the two windows don't overlap.
- rising if change > factor, falling if change < 1/factor, otherwise steady.
- Tested on the Berlin weekly series, 2024-W14 … 2026-W41, unsmoothed weekly values.

**Hindsight comparison.** *(Caveat added 2026-11-01, review finding C12: the hindsight reference is a smoothed version of the same data the rule uses, so agreement is partly built in. "Never the wrong direction" is weak evidence, not proof. The flip-flop counts don't depend on the hindsight and are the more meaningful numbers.)* The direction of a centered 5-week mean over the same span. It uses future weeks, so it's for evaluation only. Spans that change by less than ×1.1 count as steady. The hindsight span differs between window lengths, so agreement isn't strictly comparable across windows.

| Window k | Factor | Rising / steady / falling | Label changes per year | Flip-flops (rising ↔ falling within 2 weeks) | One-week labels | Agrees with hindsight | Opposite to hindsight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1.25 | 28 / 46 / 26% | 12.9 | 9 | 8 | 71% | 0% |
| 2 | 1.5 | 16 / 67 / 17% | 10.9 | 3 | 5 | 56% | 0% |
| 3 | 1.25 | 33 / 35 / 32% | 9.4 | 6 | 7 | 82% | 2.4% |
| 3 | 1.5 | 24 / 53 / 23% | 8.2 | 1 | 3 | 67% | 0.8% |

**Example, proposed rule (k = 2, ×1.25), June–October 2026.**
- It labelled the August–September 2026 rise "rising" for 8 consecutive weeks, then "steady" at the plateau from late September.
- In late June it labelled "rising" (×3.2) from weeks that included below-LOQ values. Those floor values are upper bounds, so a trend computed from them is unreliable.

### 2026-11-01: Publication delay and nowcast (`analysis/nowcast.py`; review finding A3)

**How old is Berlin's newest sample at publication?**
- *Method:* the GitHub history of `amelag_einzelstandorte.tsv` (122 commits, 2024-03-13 … 2026-10-28). Every second commit was downloaded with an HTTP range request for the first 6.5 MB, which contains the Berlin rows. 39 releases (2025-02-12 … 2026-10-28) had all three Berlin plants within the range; the older releases didn't, because of a different file layout, and were skipped.
- *Release rhythm:* weekly, on Wednesdays (commit messages "Update YYYY-MM-DD").
- *Delay* (release date minus newest Berlin SARS-CoV-2 sample): **median 10 days** (10th–90th percentile 9–13, minimum 7, maximum 20). The three plants usually report together (delay until all three: median 10).
- *Unusually slow releases:* 2026-06-10 (20 days), **2026-10-28 (17 days, the release used in this project)**, 2026-01-07 (17 days, after the holidays), 2026-04-15 (14 days, Easter).
- *Implication:* on a random day the app's newest Berlin data are ~10–16 days old (release delay plus 0–6 days since the last Wednesday), i.e. typically **2 weeks** behind in weekly terms, sometimes 3.

**Can a simple projection bridge the gap?** The trailing 2-week level was predicted h weeks ahead from week t, on 126–128 weeks of the calibration period. Methods:
- *persistence:* no change;
- *trend:* T + h·g, with g = half the 2-week change, i.e. the growth behind the trend rule;
- *damped trend:* T + (φ + φ² + … + φ^h)·g, with φ ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.85}.

Categories use the five bands in wastewater units. The table below is from the first run, with the original bands and the all-weeks fit.

**Re-run 2026-11-01 with the widened bands and the final level rule.** The errors are unchanged, since they don't depend on the bands; category accuracy and large misses change:

| Horizon | Method | Category correct | Off by ≥ 2 bands |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 wk | persistence / damped 0.7 | 87% / 86% | 0.8% / 0% |
| 2 wk | persistence / damped 0.7 | 78% / 80% | 5.5% / 3.1% |
| 3 wk | persistence / damped 0.7 | 70% / 76% | 11.1% / 5.6% |

In the 70 weeks when the trend rule says rising or falling, at 2 weeks: 76% (persistence) vs 79% (damped 0.7) correct. Damped 0.7 remains the choice: φ = 0.85 is marginally better at 3 weeks (79%) but not elsewhere.

*First run (original bands, all-weeks fit):*

| Horizon | Method | Median error | 90th pct error | Category correct | Off by ≥ 2 bands |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 wk | persistence | ×1.16 | ×1.44 | 85% | 0% |
| 1 wk | damped 0.7 | ×1.11 | ×1.39 | 88% | 0% |
| 2 wk | persistence | ×1.30 | ×2.03 | 80% | 5.5% |
| 2 wk | trend | ×1.25 | ×1.95 | 82% | 2.4% |
| 2 wk | **damped 0.7** | **×1.23** | **×1.87** | **82%** | **0.8%** |
| 3 wk | persistence | ×1.54 | ×2.65 | 72% | 9.5% |
| 3 wk | **damped 0.7** | **×1.32** | **×2.39** | **79%** | **4.0%** |

- *Result:* damped trend with φ = 0.7 is best or near-best at every horizon. Its main benefit is fewer large misses (2-band misses at 2 weeks: 5.5% → 0.8%; at 3 weeks: 9.5% → 4.0%). The gain in exact category matches is modest (+2 to +7 points).
- *Weeks with the trend rule saying rising or falling* (70 weeks): at 2 weeks, 76% (persistence) vs 77% (damped 0.7) correct. The projection doesn't help much exactly when things move, but it avoids big misses.
- *Caveats:*
  - φ was chosen from 3 candidates on the same data (mild in-sample optimism).
  - The latest data week can be incomplete (fewer samples), which wasn't modelled.
  - Beyond 3 weeks, nothing was tested.
- *Status:* how to display the projection vs the measured value is a user decision (pending).

### 2026-11-01: Wachter threshold re-check (review finding B5)

**What Wachter actually wrote** (verified: Washington Post column republished by HeraldNet, dated Sunday 20 Feb 2022, "How a vaxxed doctor judged his covid risk at a party"; the handoff's "18 Feb 2022" is probably the Post's online date, not verified):
- *Threshold:* "case rates of approximately 10 per 100,000 people per day and test positivity rates of less than 1 or 2 percent"; then "I'll begin eating indoors and will remove my mask in low-risk indoor settings. But I'll still mask up in very crowded indoor spaces."
- *No conversion to prevalence.* He gives no multiplier for unreported cases. The handoff's 2–6× factor (central 4×) is our own addition.
- *His own risk arithmetic* starts from test positivity, not case rates: at a Florida party (local rates 30–80 cases/100k/day, positivity 17%) he "adjusted downward" for vaccinated, rapid-tested guests to "about 1 in 50" per person.

**How many true infections per reported US case in early 2022?**
- *CDC serology study* (PubMed 39822271; residual commercial-lab sera, anti-nucleocapsid antibodies, Dec 2021–Feb 2022, abstract only): infections per reported case 2.2 (age 70+) to 2.9 (50–69) in adults, 3.7 in children. These count first infections only.
- *Own calculation:* national anti-N seroprevalence rose from 33.5% to 57.7% (Clarke et al., MMWR 2022;71(17), verified), i.e. ~80M first infections (× 332M). OWID reports 23–26M US cases for 2021-11-21/12-01 to 2022-01-29/02-05. Ratio **×3.1–3.5**.
- *Both are lower bounds:* reinfections aren't counted, and anti-N antibodies are detected less often after breakthrough infections in vaccinated people (known qualitatively; magnitude not checked). Home testing grew through spring 2022, so ascertainment probably worsened when cases fell to Wachter's level. The US average reached ~9/100k/day in late March 2022.
- *Wachter's own party numbers imply* (with 7 days infectious) that at 30–80 cases/100k/day the general population had at least ~2% infected, i.e. ≥ ×3.6–9.5 true infections per reported case.
- **Conclusion: 4× is supported as a central value;** if anything, the case-rate route alone would justify a bit more (~4–6×).

**But the second condition pulls the other way.**
- Test positivity under 1–2% means testing was extensive, so a larger share of infections was caught (lower multiplier) than in a typical Feb 2022 week (Maryland: 2.9% at 12/100k/day; Florida 17%).
- *Chiu & Ndeffo-Mbah (PLOS Comput Biol 2021):* undiagnosed prevalence ≈ (positivity)^(1−n) × (case rate)^n, with n = ½ (geometric mean), calibrated on US serology. Equation bodies weren't readable; the form is inferred from the text.
  - Reading the case rate as daily cases per capita, plus 10 days of diagnosed active infection: at 10/100k/day and positivity 1–2%, prevalence ≈ 0.20–0.24%. Not verified against the paper's equations.

**Net.** Three routes give roughly **0.2–0.4%** for Wachter's threshold, with 0.28% in the middle:
- case rate × 4: 0.28%;
- case rate × 4–6: 0.28–0.42%;
- with the positivity condition, via Chiu & Ndeffo-Mbah: ~0.2–0.25%.

The existing "Right around the threshold" band (0.22–0.35%) covers most of that range. **No change proposed to 0.28% or the bands.** The positivity condition is now reflected in the reasoning rather than dropped; it can't be applied directly, because there's no comparable test positivity today (sentinel positivity is among symptomatic GP patients).

**Days infectious (verified algebra).** Wachter's prevalence = 10/100k/day × days infectious × 4, and ours scales the same way, so days infectious cancels in the category; it affects only "1 in N". **To keep this true if days infectious is ever changed:** define the threshold in the config as 10/100k/day × `days_infectious` × 4, never as a hard-coded 0.28%. To implement in the runtime module.

- *Independent support covers winter only:* in England's survey (Nov 2023 – Mar 2024) every week was ≥ 0.71%, i.e. Well above. Spring to autumn rests on our estimates alone.
- *Meaning:* 0.28% ≈ 1 in 360 people; in a room of 100 people there's ~24% chance that at least one is infectious.

**Part artifact, NYC-specific.**
- *2025/26 was a mild season in NYC:* median trailing 2-week wastewater 11,683 copies/L, vs 49,191–78,094 in the other seasons. Hospital admissions only about halved (NHSN NY state 156 → 70 per 100k).
- *Not a lab change:* NYC copies/L relative to CDC WVAL for the same plant-weeks stayed at ×0.77–1.0 from 2024Q2 to 2026Q4.
- *So:* hospital totals overstate infections in mild seasons, and the latest-season rule gave NYC a level ×2.5 the earlier seasons'. Season levels at 100k copies/L: 1.56% (2023/24), 1.40% (2024/25), 3.56% (2025/26).

**Decision (user, 2026-11-01).** For NYC and DC, the level is the average (in log10) of all complete seasons' levels, each season counting equally. Berlin keeps its latest-season rule: its reference doesn't have this bias, and the latest season was the user's cautious choice (finding B7). *Superseded later the same day:* Berlin was switched to the same average rule (next entry).
