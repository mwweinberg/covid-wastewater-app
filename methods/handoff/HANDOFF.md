## 1. Required corrections (must-fix): the app currently shows wrong numbers

### 1.1 Comparisons span lab-method changes in all three cities (S1, R2, R3)

Percentile, the 75th-percentile "high mark", the alert, the history chart and the year-overlay chart currently use every week on record. All three cities changed lab method, and older values aren't comparable.

**Effect today** (figures from the methods-project audit):

| City | Lab change | Older values | App percentile today | Comparable weeks only | Weeks on ALERT, app vs comparable only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Berlin | 2024-04-03 (`laborwechsel`) | 20–40× lower | 66th | 43rd | high mark ~210k vs ~551k copies/L; 90% of weeks above the app's high mark are after the change |
| NYC | RT-qPCR → digital PCR, April 2023 | 16.5× lower (279 samples tested both ways) | 84th, "Alert: high period" | 72nd | 37% vs 21% of weeks since April 2023 |
| DC (Blue Plains) | Lab switched to Verily, 2024-06-15 | WVAL 2.7–5× regional sites since, ~1× before | ~30th | 15th | 33% vs 18% of weeks since June 2024 |

**Fix:** the feed carries `comparableSince` per city and pathogen. Everything in the app uses only weeks from that date, and **data before it isn't shown at all** (user decision):

| City | COVID | Flu A / B, RSV |
|---|---|---|
| Berlin | 2024-04-03 | start of series |
| NYC | 2023-04-16 | start of series |
| DC | 2024-06-15 | start of series |

The values are also in `threshold_config.json` → `cities.*.comparable_since`.

### 1.2 Berlin COVID series built from the wrong column (R1, R5, R6)

**Now:** a population-weighted geometric mean of RKI's `vorhersage` (their smoothing), with raw `viruslast` as the dots. The below-detection check uses `unter_bg` only.

**Fix (done by `wt.berlin_series`):**
- Use `viruslast_normalisiert`.
- Per plant and ISO week, take the mean of log10; subtract the frozen plant offset; take the population-weighted mean; then the trailing 2-week mean.
- A sample is below LOQ if `unter_bg == "ja"` **or** raw `viruslast` == 1000 (floor values RKI doesn't flag). Below-LOQ weeks still get a category from their value; the flag only hides the trend.

### 1.3 NYC COVID series mixes lab methods (N-a, N-a2)

**Now:** every row with `copies_l` goes into one geometric mean, so overlap periods count both methods. Nov 2022–Apr 2023 mixes RT-qPCR and digital PCR (616 plant-days have two rows), which creates a false jump from ~13k to ~84k copies/L in the week ending 2022-11-06.

**Fix (done by `wt.nyc_series`):**
- Digital PCR only, from 2023-04-16, with one row per plant and sample date (`dPCR-SC2` preferred over `dPCR - N1`).
- **US weeks (Sunday–Saturday, labelled by the Saturday)**, not ISO weeks.
- Per plant mean of log10, minus the frozen plant offset, population-weighted, then the trailing 2-week mean. The band edges apply only to this series.
- **Weeks after NYC Open Data's newest week are filled from CDC's WVAL for the same plants**, translated to copies/L per plant, and marked `provisional`. NYC Open Data updates about monthly; CDC weekly. Tested accuracy at city level: ×1.03 typical, ×1.14 at the 90th percentile, ×1.38 worst.

### 1.4 CDC category cut-offs wrong for flu A and RSV (S2, W1)

**Now:** `build_data.py` (`CDC_*_CUTS`) and `Analytics.cdcCategory()` hard-code cut-offs. COVID's are right; flu A and RSV are wrong, so those categories are overstated in NYC and DC.

Correct values (verified against CDC's method page and the `site_wval_category` CDC assigns in its data):

| Virus | CDC, Aug 2026 (upper bounds of Very Low / Low / Moderate / High) | App |
|---|---|---|
| COVID | 2.6 / 4.9 / 7.9 / 11.6 | same ✔ |
| Influenza A | 2.4 / 5.5 / 10.2 / 15.6 | 1.6 / 3.1 / 5.0 / 7.6 ✘ |
| RSV | 1.7 / 3.4 / 5.4 / 8.1 | 1.6 / 2.6 / 3.8 / 5.6 ✘ |

**Fix:**
- *DC:* use CDC's own `site_wval_category`.
- *NYC:* use `wt.cdc_category_nyc()`, the median WVAL of the 14 plants in the latest CDC week with at least 10 reporting, against `cdc_category_cutoffs_2026_08` in the config. Show which week it refers to; CDC's latest week often has only 8 of 14 plants.
- Remove the hard-coded cut-offs. CDC re-evaluates them every August.

### 1.5 Flu A / RSV at CDC's floor (S3)

78% of flu A and 75% of RSV site-weeks are exactly 1.0, CDC's minimum. There the app shows "higher than 0% of weeks", "+0%/week" and a 12-week-low ratio of 1.0. **Fix:** at WVAL ≤ 1.0, show "at CDC's minimum level" instead of a percentile and growth figure.

### 1.6 Lab-change and data-check alarms (R4, N-d, D-d, D-e)

**The module withholds the COVID readout and returns a message when any of these happens:**

| Status | Trigger |
|---|---|
| `LAB_CHANGE_ALARM` | Berlin: a new `laborwechsel == "ja"` after 2024-04-03. NYC: an unknown `technology` value. DC: a new CDC `source` (testing lab) for Blue Plains. |
| `METHOD_CHECK` (NYC) | NYC's copies/L shift by more than ×1.5 against CDC's index for the same plants (a silent method change), or CDC's NYC history changes beyond a rescaling. |
| `RESTATEMENT_CHECK` (DC) | CDC rewrote DC's history in a way that isn't a pure rescaling. Ordinary restatements (every 1 April / 1 October) are absorbed automatically. |

**This must be loud** (user requirement): show a prominent banner in place of the readout with the module's `message`, and send a notification. The old analytics must not keep producing a COVID alert from non-comparable data. Fixing it requires a refit in the methods project.

## 2. New readout (all three cities)

### 2.1 What the user sees

**Main line:** one of five categories relative to Wachter's reference threshold (≈0.28% of people infectious):
- "Well below the threshold"
- "Just below the threshold"
- "Right around the threshold"
- "Just above the threshold"
- "Well above the threshold"

Additions to the main line:
- Berlin, when projected: " (estimated for today)".
- NYC: the confidence label "Very low confidence". DC: "Low confidence". Berlin has none.

**Second line:** "About 1 in N people infectious · <trend> · Higher than X% of weeks since <Month Year>".
- *N:* two significant figures; "fewer than 1 in 10,000" / "more than 1 in 20" at the extremes.
- *Trend:* "rising", "falling" or "steady", or left out (see 2.2).
- *X:* percentile among comparable-period weeks.

The module returns the line ready-made as `second_line`.

**"As of":** "based on samples through <as_of>". Also show:
- "data delayed" when `data_delayed`;
- "provisional (from CDC)" when `provisional` (NYC);
- "based on one week after an N-week gap" when `single_week_after_gap`.

Today's readings (2026-11-01, from `test_against_pipeline.py`):

| City | Main line | Second line | Flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Berlin | Just below the threshold (estimated for today) | About 1 in 520 people infectious · steady · Higher than 42% of weeks since April 2024 | — |
| NYC | Well above the threshold · Very low confidence | About 1 in 77 people infectious · Higher than 73% of weeks since April 2023 | data delayed (4 weeks), provisional, no trend |
| DC | Right around the threshold · Low confidence | About 1 in 450 people infectious · Higher than 21% of weeks since June 2024 | single week after a 5-week gap, no trend |

### 2.2 Rules (the module implements all of them)

- **One number throughout:** category, "1 in N", percentile and alert all use the same value (user rule: decisions use the best guess shown).
- **Projection to today:**
  - *Berlin:* damped trend (φ = 0.7) over the weeks between the newest data week and the current week. None if that's more than 3 weeks: then the measured value is shown with "data delayed".
  - *NYC/DC:* no projection. The last value is carried forward (tested best), with "data delayed" beyond 3 weeks.
- **Trend:** mean of the latest 3 weeks vs the 3 before, ×1.25 cut-off, from measured weeks. Left out if any of the 6 weeks is missing, below LOQ (Berlin) or provisional (NYC).
- **After a gap** (previous week missing): the single newest week is used, with no trend and no projection.
- **Alert (option A; F5, N-e):** for COVID, the threshold replaces the percentile "high mark".
  - The module returns `alert_level_ok`: the level is "Right around" or higher now, or (Berlin only) projected to reach it within 3 weeks.
  - In `Analytics.kt`: **ALERT** = the existing momentum/onset rule says a rise is under way (`Phase.RISING`) **and** `alert_level_ok`. **WATCH** = RISING and not `alert_level_ok`.
  - The other phases are unchanged. The percentile high mark is no longer used for COVID.
  - If the readout is withheld (an alarm), use the alarm instead of an alert.

### 2.3 Charts (F3, F4; keep the existing chart styles)

- **Chart 1, with the main line (`LevelChart` style):** weekly best-guess prevalence from `comparableSince` (`wt.chart_series()` → `prevalence`).
  - y-axis labelled "1 in N";
  - the five bands as faint horizontal background stripes, edges from `band_edges_prevalence`;
  - Berlin: a dashed segment from the last measurement to today's projection;
  - NYC: provisional weeks marked.
- **Chart 2, with the second line (`YearOverlayChart` style):** comparable period only. Trailing 2-week wastewater values (`chart_series()` → `value`; copies/L for Berlin and NYC, WVAL for DC), current year highlighted.
- **Existing history chart:** plots the corrected series (`value`), comparable period only. Lab markers aren't needed inside the period.

### 2.4 Widget

The category plus "1 in N", e.g. "Right around · 1 in 300", plus the confidence label for NYC/DC. The existing number and sparkline stay, on the corrected series.

## 3. Integration in `pipeline/build_data.py`

The module takes the rows `build_data.py` already downloads (dicts of strings):

```python
import datetime as dt, wastewater_threshold as wt
CFG = wt.load_config()                      # pipeline/threshold_config.json
TODAY = dt.datetime.now(dt.timezone.utc).date()

def threshold_block(series):
    """Everything the app needs for COVID in one city."""
    r = wt.readout(series, CFG, TODAY)
    return {"comparableSince": str(series["comparable_since"]), "readout": r,
            "chart": wt.chart_series(series, CFG) if r.get("available") else []}

# build_berlin: rows = csv.DictReader(... amelag_einzelstandorte.tsv ...), all columns
berlin_covid = threshold_block(wt.berlin_series(rows, CFG))
# build_nyc: nyc_rows = socrata_all(NYC_API, "copies_l IS NOT NULL", ...)   (sample_date, wrrf_name, copies_l, technology)
#            cdc = the CDC NY rows already fetched (site, site_wval, week_end, pathogen_target, source)
nyc_covid = threshold_block(wt.nyc_series(nyc_rows, cdc, CFG))
cat, week, n = wt.cdc_category_nyc(cdc, CFG, "covid")       # also "fluA", "rsv" (S2)
# build_dc: rows = cdc_rows("state_territory='District of Columbia' AND site='ID:300'", args)
dc_covid = threshold_block(wt.dc_series(rows, CFG))
```

**Feed changes:**
- Add `"threshold": threshold_block(...)` to the COVID pathogen object of each city.
- Add `comparableSince` to every pathogen.
- Replace the COVID `weekly` series with the corrected one from `chart_series`: `value` = trailing 2-week value, `raw` = that week's own value. Drop weeks before `comparableSince`.

**`Model.kt`:** add the matching `@Serializable` fields, all optional, so old feeds still parse. **`Analytics.assess()`:** filter `weekly` to `weekEnd >= comparableSince` before anything else.

**Projection date:** the readout is computed for the build date (twice daily in GitHub Actions). If the app shows an older feed, label the readout with the feed's `generatedAt` date.

## 4. Configuration and refits

- **`threshold_config.json`:** generated by `pipeline/export_app_config.py` in the methods project. The app never refits (C3). The threshold is computed from its parts (10 cases/100k/day × 7 days × 4), never hard-coded (C4).
- **Refits:** routine refit in late October 2027, earlier only after an alarm. A refit produces a new config file; replace the old one. Two checks in the methods project:
  - *~April 2027:* the 2026/27 season's level; also the first real CDC restatement (1 April).
  - *Every August:* CDC's category cut-offs (`cdc_category_cutoffs_2026_08`).

## 5. Method note (short)

**What it computes**, per city: best-guess prevalence (share of people infectious) = 10^(a + b·(w − w_ref)), with w the log10 trailing 2-week wastewater value. Then the category against threshold × [½, 1/1.4, 1.4, 2] = 0.14 / 0.20 / 0.39 / 0.56%.

| | Berlin | NYC | DC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wastewater w | copies/L, normalised, 3 plants | copies/L, 14 plants | CDC WVAL, Blue Plains |
| Reference used for fitting | GrippeWeb respiratory illness × sentinel COVID positivity ÷ 0.60 symptomatic | NYC hospital admissions ÷ Berlin's infection hospitalisation risk per season | DC/MD/VA hospital admissions (Blue Plains capacity weights; MD+VA rate for the DC share) ÷ same |
| Slope b | 1.22 (fitted) | 1 (fixed) | 1 (fixed, on log WVAL) |
| Level | average of complete seasons: 0.140% at 100k copies/L | average of seasons: 1.78% at 100k copies/L (seasons 1.40–2.59%) | average of seasons: 0.180% at WVAL 3.16 (0.160–0.202%) |
| Band edges | 100k / 134k / 233k / 312k copies/L | 7.9k / 11.2k / 22.0k / 31.5k copies/L | WVAL 2.47 / 3.52 / 6.90 / 9.86 |
| Week-to-week scatter around the line | ×1.41 | ×1.6 | ×1.9 |
| Category right, at usual data age | 77% (3 weeks, projected) | 65% (4 weeks) | 58% (2 weeks) |
| Confidence label | — | Very low | Low |

**Main assumptions:**
- *Berlin's level* was checked against England's random-sample survey (winter 2023/24 totals and peak).
- *NYC/DC* assume US hospitalisation per infection equals Berlin's. That's unchecked; known remaining biases (suburban and non-resident patients, differences in hospital counting) probably make NYC/DC read somewhat high.
- *Days infectious (7)* cancels out of the category; it affects only "1 in N".

**Invalidated by:** a lab or method change (alarm), a large change in the virus or immunity (the 2027 refit checks season drift), or CDC changing its index method (alarm).
